# Pathways Shadowing Network — Operating Playbook

**Purpose:** Make meaningful, safe, equitable career-shadowing experiences easier to access for Pathways students by coordinating vetted hosts, transparent requirements, student preparation, and follow-through.

**Status:** Pilot protocol. This document is an operating template, not legal advice or a substitute for host-site policy.

## 1. Operating model

The network has three connected roles:

- **Student:** explores careers, submits an interest/request through the approved school channel, completes assigned preparation, and follows host rules.
- **Host partner / professional:** confirms whether observation is permitted, defines scope and requirements, provides an approved supervisor, and confirms the placement.
- **Pathways coordinator:** recruits and verifies partners, screens requests for fit, obtains school/guardian approvals where required, coordinates host paperwork, checks readiness, and closes the loop.

Pathways Radar is the public discovery and guidance layer. It must not become a repository for student records or host credentialing documents.

## 2. Opportunity types

Use explicit labels so expectations are clear:

1. **Career conversation:** 20–30-minute informational conversation; no clinical observation.
2. **Workplace tour:** group visit to an approved non-clinical area.
3. **Simulation / skills observation:** observe a simulation, lab, or teaching demonstration.
4. **Job shadow:** time-limited observation of a professional in an approved setting.
5. **Structured pathway placement:** recurring or longer experience that requires a separate agreement and formal oversight.

Do not describe a tour or conversation as clinical shadowing. Never imply that students can provide care, access records, enter restricted areas, or participate in clinical tasks.

## 3. Partner recruitment and vetting

Prioritize a mix of settings and roles so students can explore more than one route into health careers:

- Hospitals and health systems
- Primary care and specialty practices
- Rehabilitation, physical and occupational therapy
- Behavioral health and counseling (observation only, with heightened privacy protections)
- Nursing, pharmacy, laboratory, imaging, respiratory therapy
- Public health, community health, EMS, and health administration
- Biomedical research, simulation, health informatics, and medical education

For each partner, obtain and verify through an authorized contact:

- Organization and department; official website; approved contact role and contact method
- Whether minors are accepted and any minimum age/grade
- Available professions, dates/windows, group size, and location
- Whether clinical observation is allowed or only non-clinical exposure
- Required school/parent permissions and host forms
- Background checks, child-protection clearances, health/immunization requirements, training, orientation, ID/badges, attire, and deadlines
- Confidentiality, patient consent, infection-prevention, photography/device, and restricted-area rules
- Accessibility arrangements and accommodations process
- Costs, transportation/parking, meal arrangements, and cancellation procedure
- Named host supervisor and escalation contact
- Date last verified and who verified it

A coordinator must confirm details with the official host contact before listing an opportunity as available. A web page mentioning a program is not proof that a current shadowing placement is open.

## 4. Student-to-placement workflow

### Stage A — Discover
Student explores approved opportunity cards and role descriptions. Each card clearly states age/grade, type of experience, location, format, cost, dates, availability status, and requirements last verified.

### Stage B — Express interest
Student completes a minimal interest form through a school-approved secure channel. Collect only what is needed to route the request: student name or school-issued identifier, school/program, grade/age band, preferred career area, preferred dates, travel constraints, accessibility/support request (optional and handled privately), and contact route. Do not collect diagnoses, full medical history, Social Security numbers, immunization records, background-check reports, or signed legal documents in the public radar.

### Stage C — Coordinator review
Coordinator checks age eligibility, educational fit, schedule, transportation, accessibility needs, and host capacity. Coordinator contacts the host and confirms the placement is actually available before telling a student it is approved.

### Stage D — Requirements plan
Coordinator creates a student-specific checklist in the institution's approved system. The host/site determines its requirements. Typical items may include:
- Student/parent or guardian permission (when required)
- School/program approval and any affiliation agreement
- Host application or visitor registration
- Confidentiality / privacy acknowledgement
- Patient consent process where relevant
- Background checks and child-protection clearances where required
- Health screening / immunization documentation where required by the host
- Infection-prevention, safety, and site-specific training
- Identification, badge, dress code, and arrival instructions
- Transportation plan and emergency contact process

**Never assume a standard packet applies to every site.** The host's current written policy controls. The coordinator confirms who must complete each item, where it is submitted, and the deadline.

### Stage E — Readiness gate
Do not schedule or send a student to a site until the coordinator has verified that all required approvals and clearances are complete in the approved system and the host has confirmed the student is cleared. The Radar can display a non-sensitive status such as “Coordinator review” or “Host confirmation pending”; it must not display clearance evidence or details.

### Stage F — Experience
Before the visit, provide a short orientation:
- Student is an observer only; no patient care, clinical tasks, advice, or unsupervised activity.
- Follow host directions at all times and remain with the assigned supervisor.
- Patient participation is only with the site's consent procedure and may be declined without pressure.
- No photography, recording, social media, personal devices, patient names, or patient information unless an explicit host policy authorizes a specific activity.
- Leave immediately if asked, if a patient declines, or if privacy/safety is uncertain.
- Report concerns to the host supervisor and Pathways coordinator.

### Stage G — Close out
Within 2–5 school days, collect a brief reflection (what surprised me, skills observed, next question, career interest change) and student feedback. Ask the host for feedback and whether they would accept future students. Record aggregate, non-sensitive program outcomes.

## 5. Student readiness and professional expectations

Students should be able to:
- Explain the purpose of shadowing and their observer-only role.
- Arrive on time in approved attire with required identification.
- Use respectful, professional communication.
- Maintain confidentiality and avoid recording or sharing identifiable information.
- Respect patient choice and site restrictions.
- Ask appropriate questions at approved times.
- Notify the coordinator promptly about schedule changes, illness, access barriers, or concerns.

Provide a brief practice session and a student-facing “what to expect” guide before each placement.

## 6. Paperwork navigation: coordinator protocol

The coordinator does not give legal advice or promise that a form satisfies a host requirement. Use this sequence:

1. Obtain the host's current official checklist and forms directly from the authorized host contact.
2. Create a requirement matrix: item, who completes it, who receives it, deadline, submission channel, status, and source/date verified.
3. Confirm minor/guardian signatures and school authorization requirements before sending forms.
4. Explain forms in plain language; identify deadlines, fees, appointment steps, and where to submit them.
5. Route sensitive documents directly to the host or approved school/health-system system. Do not email unencrypted documents or upload them to GitHub, GitHub Issues, this static site, or shared spreadsheets.
6. Track only completion status and date in the coordinator-approved system. Do not retain copies unless the institution explicitly authorizes and secures them.
7. Verify receipt and clearance with the host. “Student submitted” is not the same as “host cleared.”
8. Communicate the confirmed schedule only after readiness gate passes.

## 7. Privacy, minors, and clinical boundaries

- Follow applicable school, district, health-system, site, and state/federal requirements; obtain institutional/legal/privacy review before launch.
- Treat all students as minors unless age is verified and policy says otherwise. Obtain parent/guardian permission where required.
- Do not promise students access to patients, records, procedures, or restricted spaces.
- Clinical observation depends on host approval, professional discretion, and the site's patient-consent process. A patient may decline or end observation.
- Behavioral health, counseling, reproductive health, substance-use, and other sensitive settings require special scrutiny; many will be inappropriate for minors or observers. Default to career conversations, simulations, or non-client-facing tours unless the host's privacy officer and leadership explicitly approve a safe model.
- Do not collect or publish student names, personal emails, dates of birth, health/immunization information, background-check results, legal documents, or patient information in this repository or the public website.
- Restrict student-level tracking to authorized staff in approved systems with role-based access, retention rules, and auditability.
- Have an incident/escalation plan for confidentiality concerns, safety issues, harassment, injury, boundary violations, or an unexpected patient disclosure. Follow host policy and notify the coordinator immediately.

## 8. Opportunity lifecycle and status vocabulary

Use consistent status values:
- **Draft:** not yet vetted; staff-only.
- **Partner outreach:** invitation sent; not an available placement.
- **Partner verified:** requirements and contact verified; may still have no slots.
- **Accepting interest:** host confirms current availability and a defined intake process.
- **Matching:** student interest under coordinator review.
- **Requirements in progress:** checklist active in secure system.
- **Awaiting host clearance:** paperwork submitted; host has not confirmed.
- **Confirmed:** host has confirmed readiness and schedule.
- **Completed:** experience occurred.
- **Paused / full / expired:** no new referrals until reverified.

Each opportunity records source URL, verification date, verifier role, age eligibility, opportunity type, requirements summary, slots/availability, and accessibility/transport notes. Reverify before each cohort and at least each term; immediately reverify when a deadline or policy changes.

## 9. Partner outreach sequence

1. Build a target list of departments and professional groups.
2. Send a concise invitation explaining the student age range, program purpose, observer-only boundaries, coordinator support, and proposed formats.
3. Offer a 15-minute scoping call.
4. Ask the partner to define eligible ages, settings, dates, capacity, safeguards, and requirements.
5. Complete vetting and written approval before listing.
6. Start with a small pilot (e.g., 3–5 students across 2–3 settings) and evaluate before scaling.

Do not promise that a named organization offers shadowing until it has expressly confirmed the arrangement.

## 10. Suggested pilot metrics

Track aggregate counts only: partner prospects contacted, partners verified, opportunities accepting interest, student interest requests, matches, students host-cleared, completed experiences, median days from interest to confirmation, cancellations, student-reported career clarity, and partners willing to host again. Break down access barriers (transport, cost, age eligibility, scheduling, accessibility) without exposing individual students.

## 11. Pilot launch checklist

- [ ] Identify an executive/program sponsor and named coordinator.
- [ ] Confirm school/district and host-site policy with legal/privacy/compliance contacts.
- [ ] Choose secure intake and student record systems.
- [ ] Create a standard partner agreement/letter and host requirements matrix.
- [ ] Recruit and vet 2–3 initial partners.
- [ ] Create age-appropriate student and guardian information sheets.
- [ ] Create readiness checklist and host-clearance confirmation step.
- [ ] Train students on confidentiality, observer-only boundaries, professionalism, and safety.
- [ ] Run a small pilot and review feedback before expansion.

## 12. Decision rule

When requirements are unclear, the coordinator pauses the match and asks the host's authorized contact. Student safety, confidentiality, and host policy take precedence over placement volume.
