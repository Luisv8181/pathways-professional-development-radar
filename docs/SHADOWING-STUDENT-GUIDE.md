# Student Guide: How to Get Ready for a Shadowing Experience

## What is shadowing?

Shadowing is a chance to observe a professional and learn what their workday is like. Some experiences are conversations or workplace tours; others may allow approved observation in a clinical or technical setting. Every site is different.

**You are an observer.** You do not provide care, perform procedures, give advice, handle patient records, or enter areas without permission.

## Your steps

1. **Explore:** Choose a career area that interests you. You do not need to know your future career yet.
2. **Tell Pathways:** Submit your interest through the coordinator's approved school channel. Share your career interests, availability, and travel limitations.
3. **Wait for a match:** A coordinator checks the age rules, timing, and host availability. Sending interest is not a placement confirmation.
4. **Complete requirements:** Follow the exact checklist for your host. It may include parent/guardian permission, school approval, visitor applications, confidentiality forms, clearances, health requirements, training, and ID. Not every site requires the same items.
5. **Wait for clearance:** Do not visit until Pathways and the host confirm in writing that you are cleared and scheduled.
6. **Prepare:** Review the location, arrival instructions, clothing, transportation, emergency contact, and questions you want to ask.
7. **Attend:** Stay with your supervisor, follow directions, protect privacy, and respect anyone who does not want an observer present.
8. **Reflect:** After the experience, write what you learned and whether the career still interests you.

## Privacy and professionalism

- Do not take photos, videos, or recordings.
- Do not post about patients, clients, or identifiable situations on social media.
- Do not look at screens, charts, records, or conversations unless the host explicitly authorizes an appropriate observation.
- Never repeat names or personal details you hear.
- If someone declines an observer, step out respectfully.
- Ask questions at appropriate times, not during private or urgent care.
- Tell your supervisor or Pathways coordinator if you feel unsafe, uncomfortable, or unsure.

## Paperwork support

Pathways can explain where a form comes from, who completes it, its deadline, and where the host requires it to be submitted. The host decides which forms are required. Sensitive paperwork must go directly to the host or an approved secure school system—not social media, public links, or ordinary shared documents.

## Questions to ask a professional

- What does a typical day look like?
- What do you enjoy most about your work?
- What parts of the job are challenging?
- What education or training helped you get here?
- What skills are important in this role?
- What do you wish you had known in high school?
- What other careers work alongside yours?

## After the visit

Write down:
- One thing that surprised me
- One skill I noticed
- One question I still have
- Whether this experience changed my career interests—and how
- One next step I want to take
